\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A single $\sim\!\SI{0.23}{mm}$ speck of debris on a Porter Hall window at Carnegie
Mellon --- roughly \SIrange{23}{28}{mm} before a sky monitor's $f/4$ entrance pupil ---
prints a flat-topped \SI{101}{px} shadow whose off-axis ellipticity ($1.095$, oriented
within \ang{9} of radial) matches rectilinear $\sec\theta = 1.12$ projection. Over
125~days (73~accepted nightly fixes), the shadow traced a straight \SI{310}{px} path
following Andrade's $t^{1/3}$ viscoelastic creep law ($\alpha = 0.34$, $R^2 = 0.989$),
turning an optical nuisance into a passive sub-millimetre mount strain gauge.
Calibrating the camera's empirical response function directly from exposure brackets
recovers linear radiance without laboratory equipment and reveals $>12\sigma$
direct-sun forward scattering in red ($k_{\mathrm{fac}} = +0.0072$) atop achromatic
geometric blocking ($t \approx 0.952$). A closed-loop Bayesian Stan~M2 pipeline and
conjugate MAP flat-field corrector reduce clean-sky linear dip from
\SI{+4.15}{\percent} to \SI{-0.12}{\percent} across RGB (evaluated against a 95-day,
\num{2450}+ triple prospective audit; live at
\href{https://proofsandreasons.io}{proofsandreasons.io}), motivating physics-first
handling of soiled robotic cameras. Code and data are public at
\href{https://github.com/simon-dedeo/pittsburgh-in-a-grain-of-sand}{github.com/simon-dedeo/pittsburgh-in-a-grain-of-sand}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The instrument and the defect}

A \SI{4}{K} camera (\num{3840}$\times$\num{2160}) images the Carnegie Mellon skyline
through a fixed window in Porter Hall. Every five minutes it captures a three-exposure
bracket (\code{br0}, \code{br1}, \code{br2}) at fixed daylight white balance, fused by
Mertens exposure fusion \citep{mertens2009exposure} and finished with a local-contrast
pass (CLAHE, $8\times 8$ tiles \citep{pizer1987adaptive,zuiderveld1994clahe}) plus
global saturation and contrast curves. Brackets are archived losslessly, so all
photometry and calibration run on raw frames; spot removal is applied to the raw
brackets \emph{before} fusion, because the enhancement stage amplifies any residual dip
roughly $2.9\times$, scene-dependently.

The camera (Elgato Facecam~4K) uses an $f/4.0$ fixed-focus lens --- \SI{21}{mm}
full-frame equivalent (\ang{90} nominal diagonal field of view) on a 1/1.8'' Sony
STARVIS~2 sensor (diagonal $\approx\SI{8.93}{mm}$, actual focal length
$f \approx \SI{4.33}{mm}$, entrance pupil $a = f/4 \approx \SI{1.08}{mm}$), focus range
\SIrange{30}{120}{cm} --- mounted close to the window pane on 2026-07-17. These
parameters govern every optical inference below (\S\ref{sec:physics}). Over four months
of continuous operation (July--November~2026), monotonic mount settling carried the
shadow from $(1189, 172)$ to $\sim(1481, 68)$, placing the centre only \SI{68}{px}
below the top edge of the sensor and demanding an edge-safe formulation for both
background estimation and real-time tracking.

\section{Radiometric calibration and Bayesian scattering inference}
\label{sec:inference}

\subsection{Empirical camera response function (CRF)}
\label{sec:crf}

Physical optical attenuation and scattering hold in \emph{linear radiance}, not encoded
8-bit display values. Consumer camera ISPs apply proprietary tone curves that depart
substantially from the nominal inverse-sRGB power law ($\gamma = 2.4$); applying a
nominal sRGB transform without calibration systematically distorts contrast across
brightness regimes and induces spurious additive offsets in photometric regressions.

Because the camera records known UVC manual exposure times $(E_0, E_1, E_2)$ at zero
gain in every 5-minute bracket triple, its true response curve can be measured directly
from sky frames without laboratory equipment. For any unclipped pair $(L_0, L_1)$
viewing the same patch of sky within \SI{1.5}{s}, the local log-slope is
$\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}} = \ln(E_1/E_0)/\ln(L_1/L_0)$. Across \num{2978} clean bracket
pairs in November~2026 (Figure~\ref{fig:crf}a), $\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}$ rises smoothly
from $\sim 1.5$--$1.6$ at $L \in [40, 80]$ to $\sim 2.2$--$2.3$ at $L \in [160, 200]$.
Assuming $\gamma = 2.4$ everywhere therefore overstates linear contrast in darker
channels by $\sim 1.5\times$.

We model each channel's forward response $v \in [0, 255] \mapsto I(v) \in [0, 1]$ with a
two-parameter toe-power law normalized to $I(0)=0$, $I(1)=1$:
\begin{equation}
  I_c(v) = \frac{\bigl(\frac{v/255 + a_c}{1 + a_c}\bigr)^{\gamma_c} - c_{0,c}}{1 - c_{0,c}},
  \qquad c_{0,c} = \left(\frac{a_c}{1 + a_c}\right)^{\gamma_c},
  \label{eq:crf}
\end{equation}
fitted nightly on \code{akdeniz} by Huber-robust minimization of
$\ln\bigl[(I_c(L_1)/I_c(L_0)) / (E_1/E_0)\bigr]$ over clean bracket pairs from the
trailing 14~days. On November~2026 data, Eq.~\eqref{eq:crf} reduces median
exposure-ratio error roughly $3\times$ relative to inverse-sRGB in blue
(\SI{4.46}{\percent} $\to$ \SI{1.74}{\percent}, $a_B = 0.678$, $\gamma_B = 4.19$) and
green (\SI{5.39}{\percent} $\to$ \SI{2.33}{\percent}, $a_G = 0.462$, $\gamma_G = 3.90$),
and from \SI{13.98}{\percent} to \SI{8.50}{\percent} in red ($a_R = 0.420$,
$\gamma_R = 3.39$; the residual red spread reflects the ISP's colour-correction matrix
on saturated blue sky). Linearizing with Eq.~\eqref{eq:crf} renders blue and green
shadow depth essentially exposure-invariant across $L \in [20, 248]$ without empirical
depth-warping curves.

\subsection{Bayesian Stan M2 scattering model}
\label{sec:bayes}

In linear radiance per channel $c \in \{B, G, R\}$, a defocused window contaminant both
attenuates background sky radiance $\Lbg$ and scatters ambient window illumination
toward the sensor \citep{gu2009dirty}:
\begin{equation}
  \Lsp = t_c\,\Lbg + k_{\mathrm{fac},c}\,\Lfac + s_{0,c},
  \label{eq:m2}
\end{equation}
where direct-sun and courtyard illumination on the glass is proxied by the simultaneous
CRF-linearized radiance $\Lfac$ of a fixed $100\times 100$ patch on the sunlit
courtyard building facade (\code{spotlab.py}). When an unconstrained constant offset
$s_{0,c}$ is included under empirical CRF linearization, posterior estimates collapse
statistically to zero in all three channels (G: $+0.0013\,[-0.0089, +0.0108]$;
R: $+0.0004\,[-0.0101, +0.0116]$; B: $-0.0102\,[-0.0251, +0.0057]$), confirming pure
multiplicative attenuation plus additive illumination-driven scattering.

Setting $s_{0,c} = 0$ and dividing by $\Lbg$ yields a dimensionless effective
transmittance $y_n = \Lsp/\Lbg$ with well-conditioned HMC geometry:
\begin{equation}
  y_n \;\sim\; \operatorname{Student-}t\!\left(\nu_{c(n)},\;
    t_{c(n)} + k_{\mathrm{fac},c(n)}\,\frac{L_{\mathrm{fac},n}}{L_{\mathrm{bg},n}},\;
    \sqrt{\sigma_n^2 + \sigma_{x,c(n)}^2}\right),
  \label{eq:stan}
\end{equation}
with weakly informative priors $1 - t_c \sim \mathrm{LogNormal}(\ln 0.05, 0.5)$,
$k_{\mathrm{fac},c} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.02)$,
$\nu_c \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(2, 0.15)$ bounded to $[2, 50]$, and
$\sigma_{x,c} \sim \mathcal{N}_+(0, 0.015)$ bounded below at $10^{-4}$ (the 8-bit
quantization floor). The Student-$t$ likelihood provides automatic robustness against
residual cloud edges \citep{lange1989robust}.

Equation~\eqref{eq:stan} is sampled nightly on \code{akdeniz} via \code{cmdstanpy}
\citep{carpenter2017stan} (4 parallel NUTS chains, \code{adapt\_delta=0.95},
$N \le 3000$ clean observations from the trailing 7~days, $\sim$\SI{45}{s}).
Table~\ref{tab:stan} and Figure~\ref{fig:crf}b report the 2026-11-24 posterior
($N = 2921$, zero divergences, $\hat{R}_{\max} = 1.0055$):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Pure optical transmittance is achromatic:} $t_B = 0.9525$,
  $t_G = 0.9526$, $t_R = 0.9474$ (area-averaged over the $r < \SI{60}{px}$ photometry
  disk; $B$ and $G$ agree to \SI{0.01}{\percent}).
\item \textbf{Direct-sun forward scattering is detected at $>12\sigma$ in red:}
  $k_{\mathrm{fac},R} = +0.00724$ (90\% CI $[+0.00665, +0.00783]$; green $+0.00352$,
  blue $+0.00415$). When direct sunlight strikes the glass and facade against the dark
  red component of clear blue sky ($\Lfac/\Lbg \approx 3$--$4$), warm forward-scattered
  light partially refills the red shadow by $\sim\SIrange{2}{3}{\percent}$; on overcast
  or low-sun frames ($\Lfac/\Lbg \lesssim 0.3$), the scatter vanishes and the full
  optical dip appears.
\item \textbf{Residuals are near-Gaussian and sub-0.1\%:} unexplained scatter
  $\sigma_{x}$ is just \SI{0.07}{\percent} (B) and \SI{0.06}{\percent} (G) with
  $\nu \in [13.5, 31.5]$ (compared to $\sigma_x \approx 0.5$--\SI{0.7}{\percent} and
  $\nu \approx 3$ under nominal inverse-sRGB linearization).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Position tracking, spatial validation, and epoch bookkeeping}
\label{sec:filter}

The nightly position estimator (\code{spotnight.py} on \code{akdeniz}) is fast and
nonparametric: it selects bright, flat-ring frames from each bracket class (\code{br0},
\code{br1}, \code{br2}) over an adaptive 2-to-4-day window (widening automatically
during overcast spells), masks the top 20~ISP edge rows (\code{TOP\_MASK = 20}),
divides each frame by a sigma-clipped ridge-regularized background fit, normalizes each
bracket's median dip by its own peak (reducing per-bracket position spread from
$\sim\SI{60}{px}$ to $\sim\SIrange{2}{6}{px}$), and computes a half-depth centroid.
A recursive filter \citep{kalman1960,sarkka2023filtering} propagates state forward
using a linear velocity fitted across the trailing 10 accepted nights (suppressing
single-night differencing noise once speed falls below \SI{1}{px/day}), rejecting steps
$> \SI{45}{px}$ or bracket spreads $> \SI{18}{px}$ in favour of carry-forward.

This estimator was independently cross-checked offline against a hierarchical pixelwise
Student-$t$ elliptical-blob model fitted in CmdStan via multi-start L-BFGS
\citep{liu1989lbfgs,papaspiliopoulos2007parametrization,betancourt2015hmc,gelman2020workflow}
(\code{offline\_july/\allowbreak spothier.stan}), with positions linked across days by a
non-centred random walk and free per-bracket amplitudes. The two estimators agreed to
$\sim\SI{3}{px}$ across an 8-day track while sharing no code or likelihood machinery.

A strict bookkeeping rule prevents systematic lag: \textbf{every position estimate
carries the mean timestamp of its constituent frames (\code{fit\_epoch}), never the
wall-clock run date}. Because a 2--4~day stack's effective epoch trails the run time by
$\sim 1.5$~days, computing velocity and downstream extrapolation against elapsed epoch
time eliminates $v\,\Delta t$ placement lag and its characteristic bright/dark dipole.

\section{The multi-bracket empirical transmittance map}
\label{sec:map}

The shadow's radial profile is flat-topped with compact support, falling far more
steeply than a Gaussian (Figure~\ref{fig:profile}a). The spatial centroid therefore
only \emph{locates}; correction shape comes from a registered per-channel empirical
transmittance map \citep{howell2006handbook},
\begin{equation}
  T_c(\mathbf{r}) \;=\; \operatorname{median}_j\,
     \frac{I_{j,c}(\mathbf{r})}{B_{j,c}(\mathbf{r})},
\end{equation}
stacked in CRF linear radiance with velocity registration (shifting each frame's window
to its interpolated centre so motion never smears the stack) and light Gaussian
denoising ($\sigma = \SI{1.5}{px}$). Three design choices keep $T_c$ accurate across
seasons and edge-clamped geometries:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Multi-bracket stacking.} Stacking only a single fixed exposure fails
  seasonally: in clear midday sky the blue channel saturates ($L > 249$), while under
  winter overcast shorter exposures become too dark. Pooling clean, unclipped frames
  across \emph{all three exposure brackets} (\code{br0/br1/br2}), uniformly sampled
  over an adaptive 3-to-6-day window with an encoded-DN flatness gate
  ($\mathrm{MAD} < 2.5$~DN), yields $[81, 98, 68]$ clean frames per channel in \SI{4}{s}
  on 8 threads without midday illumination bias.
\item \textbf{Top-edge ridge regularization and \code{TOP\_MASK = 20}.} At $y \approx 68$,
  the upper half of an outer sky annulus lies off the sensor, making an unregularized
  2D quadratic background fit ill-conditioned in $y^2$ and prone to extrapolating cloud
  edges into the core. Tightening the background annulus to $r \in [85, 115]$~px (where
  Figure~\ref{fig:profile}a shows optical deficit is already $< \SI{0.06}{\percent}$),
  masking $y < 20$, and applying a ridge penalty $\mathrm{diag}(0, 0, 0, 50, 50, 200)$
  to the quadratic terms whenever $y < r_1 + 20$ stabilizes edge extrapolation cleanly.
\item \textbf{Core-independent sky selection.} Following lucky-imaging selection-bias
  principles \citep{law2006lucky,smith2009lucky}, frames are gated strictly on ring
  flatness and unclipped brightness outside the footprint, never on core depth.
\end{itemize}

\section{Live Bayesian MAP correction}
\label{sec:apply}

Every 5~minutes on \code{pandr}, \code{flatfield.py} corrects the three raw HDR
brackets in CRF linear radiance before Mertens fusion:

\paragraph{1. Placement (prediction + symmetric lock).}
The bundle's published position is extrapolated from \code{fit\_epoch} by velocity
(clamped to $[-0.5, 3.5]$~days and $\le \SI{40}{px}$). On daylight HDR calls
(median $> 30$~DN), a single normalized cross-correlation template lock
\citep{lewis1995ncc} runs on the brightest unclipped bracket using a \emph{symmetric}
vertical search bound $r_y = \min(25, \max(0, y - 48))$ so top-edge proximity never
biases the search downward; decisive locks ($\mathrm{NCC} \ge 0.50$, rival-peak margin
$\ge 0.08$) are smoothed over a trailing 1-hour window.

\paragraph{2. Conjugate Bayesian MAP depth across brackets.}
From the placed map $T_c$, we extract the normalized 2D spatial shape
$p_c(\mathbf{r}) = \mathrm{clip}(1 - T_c(\mathbf{r}), 0, 0.25)\,w(\mathbf{r}) / d_{0,c}$
(feathered smoothly to zero outside $r = \SI{82}{px}$ and normalized so
$\mathrm{median}_{\mathrm{core}}(p_c) = 1$). Because the blemish's optical shape
$p_c$ is fixed while its effective depth $d_c$ varies with sunlight scattering
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:stan}), each channel $c$ admits a closed-form conjugate MAP estimate:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Physical prior from Stan~M2:} reading the sunlit facade patch $\Lfac$ on
  the current frame gives
  $d_{\mathrm{prior},c} = s_{\mathrm{core},c}\bigl((1 - t_c) - k_{\mathrm{fac},c}\,\Lfac/\Lbg\bigr)$,
  with prior variance $\tau_c^2 = \max(3\,s_{\mathrm{core},c}\,\sigma_{x,c},\; 0.022)^2$.
  Even under heavy cloud texture, the prior already predicts the direct-sun scatter
  contribution.
\item \textbf{Matched-filter likelihood across unclipped brackets:} on every unclipped
  bracket $i$ with ring roughness $\rho_i = \mathrm{MAD}_i/\mathrm{median}(B_i) < 0.08$,
  the optimal linear projection of the observed deficit onto $p_c$ is
  $\hat d_{i,c} = \langle p_c, 1 - I_{i,c}/B_{i,c}\rangle / \langle p_c, p_c\rangle$,
  with variance $\sigma_{i,c}^2 = (0.40\,\rho_i)^2 + 0.003^2$.
\item \textbf{Conjugate posterior and clipped-bracket inheritance:} pooling across
  unclipped brackets gives
  $d_{\mathrm{pool},c} = \bigl(d_{\mathrm{prior},c}/\tau_c^2 + \sum_i \hat d_{i,c}/\sigma_{i,c}^2\bigr) / \bigl(1/\tau_c^2 + \sum_i 1/\sigma_{i,c}^2\bigr)$,
  bounded safely to $[-0.015, 0.14]$ (allowing slight bright-scatter fill-in without
  runaway gain; applied transmittance clamped to $[0.84, 1.02]$). Partially clipped
  brackets (e.g.\ \code{br1} where surrounding sky clips at 255 while the shadowed core
  sits at 241) inherit $d_{\mathrm{pool},c}$ from the unclipped brackets rather than
  being skipped. For low-level red ($L < 120$), where the ISP colour matrix introduces
  mild bracket-specific curvature, each clean bracket shrinks gently toward its own
  $\hat d_{i,R}$.
\end{itemize}
All safety bounds are strictly \emph{model-referenced}, never scene-referenced, so
bright clouds cannot trigger false attenuation.

\paragraph{3. Quantization and post-processing measurement considerations.}
Two low-level signal-processing effects strongly influence both correction accuracy and
auditing near frame boundaries. First, when converting linear-space corrected pixels
back to 8-bit integers, nearest-integer rounding (\code{np.rint()}) is essential:
flooring via \code{astype(np.uint8)} introduces a systematic $0.5$--$1.0$~DN darkening
bias across the footprint, translating to $\sim\SIrange{0.7}{1.5}{\percent}$ in linear
radiance and $\sim\SIrange{2}{3}{\percent}$ after local-contrast enhancement. Second,
on \SI{4}{K} frames, $8\times 8$ CLAHE places a vertical tile seam at $x = 1440$
(immediately beside $x = 1481$) and transitions from clamped border tiles to bilinear
interpolation at $y = 135$ (between the $y = 68$ core and the lower background ring at
$y \approx 173$--$198$). On a synthetic zero-defect smooth sky, this tile geometry alone
creates a \SIrange{+3.0}{+3.9}{\percent} phantom post-CLAHE dip under a one-sided
ring metric. Quantitative verification (\code{spot\_ab.py}) therefore measures
\textbf{pre-CLAHE Mertens linear dips per channel} alongside post-enhancement display
dips.

\section{Two-tier autonomous architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

Inference and real-time correction are separated cleanly across two hosts: a nightly
batch pipeline on a compute server (\code{spotnight.py} + \code{spot\_m2.stan} on
\code{akdeniz}) and a lightweight closed-form corrector on the capture host
(\code{flatfield.py} on \code{pandr}). Only the \SI{1.1}{MB} nightly model bundle
(\code{spot\_model.json} schema~3 + \code{spot\_tmap.npy}) crosses the network via
atomic swap; the live 5-minute capture loop has zero per-tick dependency on the compute
server, and raw brackets are archived untouched so calibration data are never
contaminated by prior corrections (Table~\ref{tab:levels}, Figure~\ref{fig:flow}).

\section{Prospective and longitudinal performance}
\label{sec:results}

Performance is audited nightly on \code{pandr} by \code{spot\_ab.py}, which replays up
to 36 sampled bracket triples from the preceding 26~hours in a throwaway sandbox under
three matched arms: uncorrected, live-corrected with template lock, and live-corrected
without lock.

Under the Bayesian CRF + Stan~M2 pipeline (\code{bayes3}; Table~\ref{tab:perf},
Figure~\ref{fig:ab}):
\begin{itemize}
\item On the live 26-hour prospective audit (2026-11-24, $n=18$ clean daylight triples),
  \textbf{pre-CLAHE Mertens linear dip} drops from \SI{+4.15}{\percent} to
  \textbf{\SI{-0.12}{\percent}} (median $|\text{after}| = \SI{0.21}{\percent}$), with
  every colour channel nulled to within a tenth of a percent
  (\textbf{B: \SI{-0.08}{\percent}, G: \SI{-0.02}{\percent}, R: \SI{-0.01}{\percent}}).
\item \textbf{Zero frames are made worse} ($0/18$ with $|\text{after}| > |\text{before}|$),
  and symmetric template lock improves residual accuracy over open-loop extrapolation
  ($-0.12\%$ vs.\ $+0.14\%$).
\item \textbf{Post-CLAHE enhanced display dip} drops from \SI{+11.89}{\percent} to
  \textbf{\SI{+0.63}{\percent}}.
\item On the synthetic end-to-end verification test
  (\code{tests/test\_synthetic\_end\_to\_end.py}), injecting the warped published
  transmittance map into a smooth 3-bracket sky nulls all three channels against exact
  ground truth to $+0.36\%$ (B), $+0.22\%$ (G), and $+0.36\%$ (R) (\code{PASS},
  threshold \SI{0.5}{\percent}).
\end{itemize}

Comparing against a 95-day prospective baseline using nominal inverse-sRGB
linearization and an empirical depth-scaling curve (\num{2450} clean daylight triples,
2026-08-17 to 2026-11-23; Figure~\ref{fig:ab} and Table~\ref{tab:perf}) quantifies the
gain from explicit physical modeling. Across the 3-month baseline, median enhanced dip
was reduced from $+12.94\%$ to $+2.53\%$ (Aug--Nov~05, $n=2016$) and $+13.60\%$ to
$+3.95\%$ as the blemish neared the top edge (Nov~09--23, $n=434$), with a pre-CLAHE
Mertens linear residual of $+0.76\%$ overall and $+2.18\%$ in red (Nov~20--23, $n=19$).
Incorporating empirical CRF calibration, Stan~M2 scatter priors, multi-bracket stacking,
edge ridge regularization, and exact rounding removes the red channel residual
completely ($+2.18\% \to -0.01\%$) and cuts the enhanced display residual by more than
$6\times$ ($+3.95\% \to +0.63\%$), matching the closed-loop offline oracle benchmark
($+0.52\%$, $n=495$ over 8~July days). Figure~\ref{fig:ba} shows pixel-scale detail of
the correction: only the compact footprint is modified, preserving local photon noise.

\section{Optical physics and viscoelastic mount metrology}
\label{sec:physics}

\subsection{Pupil-shadow geometry and off-axis ellipticity}

For a camera of entrance-pupil diameter $a \approx \SI{1.08}{mm}$ focused at nominal
distance $F \approx \SI{0.5}{m}$, the ray cone collected by a single sensor pixel
intersects a window at distance $d$ in front of the entrance pupil in a circular patch
of diameter $a' = a(1 - d/F)$ \citep{goodman2017fourier,potmesil1981lens,willson2005dust}.
An opaque speck of diameter $s < a'$ convolved with that circular pupil produces a
\emph{flat-topped} shadow whose central fractional attenuation is $(s/a')^2$ and whose
\textbf{exact half-maximum diameter equals the projected pupil diameter $a'$ itself}
(to $O((s/a')^2)$), not $a' + s$.

Across four months of data, the empirical flat-top width is $\mathrm{FWHM} = \SI{101}{px}$
($r_{1/2} = 51.3 / 50.3 / 48.9\text{ px}$ in B/G/R, stable to \SI{0.4}{px};
Figure~\ref{fig:profile}a). With paraxial focal length
$f_{\mathrm{px}} = \sqrt{3840^2 + 2160^2}/2 \approx \SI{2203}{px}$, at the blemish's
off-axis angle $\theta = \arctan(1103 / 2203) = \ang{26.6}$ ($\sec\theta = 1.118$,
geometric-mean area stretch $\sec^{3/2}\theta = 1.182$):
\begin{equation}
  d \;\approx\; \frac{g_{\theta}\,f_{\mathrm{px}}\,a}{\mathrm{FWHM}_{\mathrm{px}} + g_{\theta}\,f_{\mathrm{px}}\,a/F}
    \;\approx\; \SIrange{22.6}{26.5}{mm} \quad (g_\theta \in [1, 1.182]),
\end{equation}
giving $d \approx \SIrange{23}{28}{mm}$ --- directly consistent with $\sim\SIrange{4}{6}{mm}$
pupil inset behind the front element of a wide-angle retrofocus lens plus the
camera-to-pane gap and double-pane glass thickness (if the speck sits on the outer
surface of Porter Hall's insulated glazing). The measured area ratio
$(s/a')^2 \approx 0.052$ with $a' \approx \SI{1.02}{mm}$ yields a speck diameter of
$s \approx \SIrange{0.23}{0.24}{mm}$ and a translational lever of
$f_{\mathrm{px}}/d \approx \SIrange{78}{97}{px/mm}$ on axis ($\sim\SIrange{95}{115}{px/mm}$
off axis).

An independent geometric prediction tests this rectilinear model directly: an off-axis
circular pupil shadow at angle $\theta$ projects onto a flat sensor as an ellipse
stretched radially by $\sec^2\theta$ and tangentially by $\sec\theta$, yielding an axis
ratio of $\sec\theta = 1.118$ oriented directly toward the optical axis $(1920, 1080)$
(radial angle \ang{66.4}). On the empirical transmittance map (Figure~\ref{fig:profile}b),
the weighted second-moment ellipse has axis ratio \textbf{1.095} oriented at
\textbf{\ang{57.7}} --- matching both aspect ratio and orientation to within \ang{8.7}.

\subsection{125-day viscoelastic mount creep: a passive optical strain gauge}

Four months of nightly tracking (73~accepted fixes spanning 125~days, 2026-07-22 to
2026-11-24; Figure~\ref{fig:creep}) reveal a remarkably clean mechanical signature:
\begin{itemize}
\item The shadow moved monotonically along a single straight line from $(1188.7, 172.4)$
  to $(1480.8, 68.0)$: $\Delta x = +292.2$~px, $\Delta y = -104.4$~px, total distance
  \SI{310.3}{px}, with an RMS perpendicular scatter of just \textbf{\SI{4.1}{px}}
  (1.3\% of path length) and \emph{zero} seasonal reversal as Pittsburgh cooled from
  July heat to November winter.
\item Its velocity decayed smoothly by nearly two orders of magnitude: $\sim\SI{14}{px/day}$
  in late July $\to \SI{3.0}{px/day}$ in August $\to \SI{1.7}{px/day}$ in September
  $\to \SI{0.8}{px/day}$ in October $\to <\SI{0.3}{px/day}$ in late November.
\item Along-axis displacement $s(t)$ is poorly described by constant-velocity drift
  ($R^2 = 0.873$, $\mathrm{RMSE} = \SI{25.9}{px}$) or single-timescale exponential
  relaxation ($R^2 = 0.919$), but closely follows \textbf{Andrade's universal $t^{1/3}$
  primary viscoelastic creep law} \citep{andrade1910creep}, $s(t) = A\,t^{\alpha}$ with
  $\alpha = \mathbf{0.339}$ ($R^2 = \mathbf{0.9889}$, $\mathrm{RMSE} = \SI{7.6}{px}$),
  and logarithmic creep $A\ln(1 + t/\tau)$ ($\tau = \SI{2.19}{d}$, $R^2 = 0.9854$,
  $\mathrm{RMSE} = \SI{8.8}{px}$).
\end{itemize}
At $\sim\SI{95}{px/mm}$, the 125-day walk corresponds to $\sim\SIrange{3.1}{3.7}{mm}$
of monotonic viscoelastic settling of the camera mount following its July~17
installation. Because distant skyline landmarks move negligibly under millimetre
translation, the accidental window grain functions as a passive
\SI{10}{\micro\meter}-resolution optical strain gauge.

\subsection{Reciprocity, scattering, and chromaticity under diffuse sky illumination}
\label{sec:reciprocity}

The measured optical attenuation and colour structure clarify how classical particle
scattering behaves in wide-field imaging against an extended sky:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Extended-sky reciprocity explains $1\times$ geometric blocking.}
  For a macroscopic particle ($s \gg \lambda$) illuminated by a \emph{collimated plane
  wave or narrow beam}, the optical extinction paradox gives a far-field extinction
  cross-section of $2\times$ the geometric area ($Q_{\mathrm{ext}} = 2$), half from
  blocking/absorption and half from forward edge diffraction
  \citep{vandehulst1981light,bohren1983absorption,berg2011extinction,deepak1978forward,lai2004beam,markel2020extinction}.
  A sky monitor, however, views an \emph{incoherent, extended diffuse sky}. By
  Helmholtz reciprocity (conservation of radiance), elastic forward diffraction out of
  a pixel's acceptance cone is compensated by rays diffracted in from adjacent sky
  directions of equal radiance, so diffraction produces no net darkening. Only
  geometric blocking ($1 - t \approx (s/a')^2 \approx 0.05$) and anisotropic
  illumination scattering ($k_{\mathrm{fac}}\Lfac$) survive.
\item \textbf{Chromaticity separates into achromatic blocking, red forward scatter, and
  mild optical dispersion.} Under empirical CRF calibration, raw daytime integrated
  deficits stand in the ratio B:G:R $= 1.000 : 1.097 : 0.904$ --- red appearing 10\%
  shallower than blue because low-angle direct sunlight elevates the red
  $k_{\mathrm{fac}}\Lfac/\Lbg$ forward-scattering fill-in. Once Stan~M2 factors out
  this illumination-driven scatter, pure optical blocking $1 - t_c$ is achromatic in
  blue and green (\SI{4.75}{\percent} vs.\ \SI{4.74}{\percent}; Table~\ref{tab:stan}).
  Separately, the shadow's radial width exhibits a reproducible \SI{2.4}{px} (5\%)
  wavelength ordering ($r_{1/2} = 51.3 / 50.3 / 48.9\text{ px}$ in B/G/R across all
  four months), consistent with modest axial or pupil chromatic aberration in the lens.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Relation to prior work}
\label{sec:literature}

The artifact-removal literature separates cleanly into static physics-based models for
lens or sensor dust \citep{willson2005dust,zhou2007dust,gu2007dirtyglass,gu2009dirty},
multi-view parallax methods requiring camera motion \citep{xue2015obstruction},
refractive raindrop models \citep{you2016adherent}, data-driven automotive soiling
segmentation/inpainting \citep{uricar2019soiling}, and population-level dust deposition
censuses on Mars \citep{chenchen2023insight}. This work contributes three results at
their intersection:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Single-grain validation of \citet{gu2009dirty} with explicit scatter
  forcing:} measuring both achromatic attenuation ($t \approx 0.952$) and direct-sun
  additive scattering ($k_{\mathrm{fac},R} = +0.0072$) per channel in an uncalibrated
  consumer HDR camera using exposure brackets alone.
\item \textbf{Continuous 125-day tracking and mount metrology:} demonstrating that even
  a rigidly mounted camera violates the calibrate-once assumption by $>3\times$ the
  shadow's FWHM through viscoelastic mount creep ($\alpha = 0.34$), while turning the
  defocused shadow into a passive sub-millimetre strain gauge.
\item \textbf{Closed-loop Bayesian deployment with prospective auditing:} showing that
  conjugate MAP shrinkage toward a live physical Stan posterior --- paired with
  empirical CRF calibration, multi-bracket stacking, and edge ridge regularization ---
  achieves sub-0.15\% linear optical removal across RGB without black-box inpainting.
\end{itemize}

\section{Code, reproducibility, and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

{\sloppy
All code, paper sources, and reproduction datasets are public in a dedicated
repository at
\url{https://github.com/simon-dedeo/pittsburgh-in-a-grain-of-sand}
(deployed alongside the broader skyline-monitor stack at
\url{https://github.com/simon-dedeo/cmu-skyline-monitor}).
Within the analysis repository, deployed production scripts live in
\code{production/akdeniz/} (\code{spotnight.py}, \code{spot\_m2.stan},
\code{spotnight\_cron.sh}) and \code{production/pandr/} (\code{flatfield.py},
\code{spot\_ab.py}, \code{spotlab.py}, \code{hdrfuse.py}, \code{nightly.sh},
\code{spot\_model.json}); offline July analysis scripts live in
\code{offline\_july/}; all datasets needed to reproduce the figures, tables,
CRF calibration, and Stan~M2 fits live in \code{data/} with a one-command
driver (\code{reproduce\_figures.py}); and the synthetic end-to-end test is
\code{tests/test\_\allowbreak synthetic\_\allowbreak end\_\allowbreak to\_\allowbreak end.py}.
\par}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Prospective window for the full CRF + Stan~M2 corrector:} while the
  prospective A/B infrastructure has accumulated \num{2450} clean daylight triples over
  95~days under the sRGB baseline, the live CRF + Stan~M2 corrector has so far been
  audited over one full daylight cycle ($n=18$ clean triples); multi-week winter
  accumulation is ongoing.
\item \textbf{Uncalibrated local plate scale and pane side:} $d \approx \SIrange{23}{28}{mm}$
  and the $\sim\SI{95}{px/mm}$ lever inherit $\sim 20\%$ geometric uncertainty until a
  controlled camera translation or physical wipe test (inner vs.\ outer pane) is
  performed.
\item \textbf{Consumer ISP red-channel nonlinearity:} even after two-parameter CRF
  calibration, red exposure ratios retain \SI{8.5}{\percent} median error from the ISP
  colour matrix on saturated blue sky; raw Bayer readout would eliminate this entirely.
\item \textbf{Top-edge margin:} at $y \approx 68$~px, the upward template-lock range is
  narrowed to \SI{20}{px}; if mount creep were to continue upward past $y < 55$,
  physically lifting the camera a few millimetres remains the only permanent remedy.
\item \textbf{Extreme distractors:} no systematic false-correction stress test has been
  run on birds, contrails, or direct solar glare sitting squarely inside the footprint,
  though Bayesian roughness shrinkage and model-referenced depth bounds
  ($[-0.015, 0.14]$) strictly cap any perturbation.
\end{itemize}
