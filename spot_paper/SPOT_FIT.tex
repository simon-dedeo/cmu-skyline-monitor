\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A single $\sim\!\SI{0.23}{mm}$ speck of debris on a Porter Hall window at Carnegie
Mellon --- roughly \SIrange{23}{28}{mm} before a sky monitor's $f/4$ entrance pupil ---
prints a flat-topped \SI{101}{px} shadow whose off-axis ellipticity ($1.095$, oriented
within \ang{9} of radial) matches rectilinear $\sec\theta = 1.12$ projection. Over
125~days (73~accepted nightly fixes), the shadow traced a straight \SI{310}{px} path
following Andrade's $t^{1/3}$ viscoelastic creep law ($\alpha = 0.34$, $R^2 = 0.989$),
turning an optical nuisance into a sub-millimetre mount strain gauge. Calibrating the
camera's empirical response function from exposure brackets eliminates a previously
puzzling dark offset and reveals $>12\sigma$ direct-sun forward scattering in red
($k_{\mathrm{fac}} = +0.0072$) atop achromatic blocking ($t \approx 0.952$) ---
revising an earlier v11 extinction-paradox interpretation. A live Bayesian Stan~M2
pipeline and conjugate MAP corrector reduce clean-sky linear dip from
\SI{+4.15}{\percent} to \SI{-0.12}{\percent} across RGB (prospective audit over
\num{2450}+ daylight triples; live at
\href{https://proofsandreasons.io}{proofsandreasons.io}), motivating physics-first
handling of soiled robotic cameras. Code and data are public at
\href{https://github.com/simon-dedeo/pittsburgh-in-a-grain-of-sand}{github.com/simon-dedeo/pittsburgh-in-a-grain-of-sand}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The instrument and the defect}

A \SI{4}{K} camera (\num{3840}$\times$\num{2160}) images the Carnegie Mellon skyline
through a fixed window in Porter Hall. Every five minutes it captures a three-exposure
bracket (\code{br0}, \code{br1}, \code{br2}) at fixed daylight white balance, fused by
Mertens exposure fusion \citep{mertens2009exposure} and finished with a local-contrast
pass (CLAHE, $8\times 8$ tiles \citep{pizer1987adaptive,zuiderveld1994clahe}) plus
global saturation and contrast curves. Brackets are archived losslessly, so all
photometry and calibration run on raw frames; spot removal is applied to the raw
brackets \emph{before} fusion, because the enhancement stage amplifies any residual dip
roughly $2.9\times$, scene-dependently.

The camera (Elgato Facecam~4K) uses an $f/4.0$ fixed-focus lens --- \SI{21}{mm}
full-frame equivalent (\ang{90} nominal diagonal field of view) on a 1/1.8'' Sony
STARVIS~2 sensor (diagonal $\approx\SI{8.93}{mm}$, actual focal length
$f \approx \SI{4.33}{mm}$, entrance pupil $a = f/4 \approx \SI{1.08}{mm}$), focus range
\SIrange{30}{120}{cm} --- mounted close to the pane on 2026-07-17. These numbers govern
every physical inference below (\S\ref{sec:physics}). By 2026-11-24, after four months
of monotonic mount creep from $(1189, 172)$ in July, the shadow sits at
$\sim(1481, 68)$ --- only \SI{68}{px} below the top edge of the sensor, an extreme
edge-clamped geometry that broke several naive assumptions of the initial July design.

\paragraph{What changed in v12 (and what is deployed).}
Version~11 (2026-07-28) reported eight days of initial operation. Four months of
continuous unattended operation (July--November~2026) and a November~2026 audit exposed
both real production failure modes (winter blue-channel clipping, an integer truncation
bug in \code{flatfield.py}, top-edge quadratic blow-up, and CLAHE tile-seam measurement
bias) and physical errors in v11's optical interpretation (notably applying collimated
plane-wave extinction-paradox arguments to a diffuse extended sky, conflating a
Gaussian fit's width with the flat-topped pupil shadow's true FWHM, and attributing
monotonic mount creep to reversible thermal breathing). This revision corrects those
errors, integrates the full 125-day dataset, and documents the simplified \code{bayes3}
architecture now deployed on \code{akdeniz} and \code{pandr}. Byte-copies of the live
files live under \code{production/}; offline validation code from July remains in
\code{spot\_paper/code/}.

\section{Radiometric and Bayesian statistical model}
\label{sec:inference}

\subsection{Empirical camera response function (CRF)}
\label{sec:crf}

An optical transmittance model holds in \emph{linear radiance}, not encoded 8-bit
display values. Earlier versions of the pipeline (v1--v11) linearized pixels with an
assumed inverse-sRGB curve ($\gamma = 2.4$), and then compensated for bracket-to-bracket
depth discrepancies with an ad-hoc 5-knot empirical depth-scaling curve
(\code{scurve}).

With known UVC manual exposure times $(E_0, E_1, E_2)$ at zero gain across every
5-minute bracket triple, the camera's actual response curve can be measured directly
without laboratory equipment. For any unclipped pair $(L_0, L_1)$ viewing the same sky
within 1.5~s, the local log-slope is $\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}} = \ln(E_1/E_0)/\ln(L_1/L_0)$.
Across \num{2978} clean bracket pairs in November~2026 (Figure~\ref{fig:crf}a), the
Facecam~4K ISP departs strongly from inverse-sRGB: $\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}$ rises
smoothly from $\sim 1.5$--$1.6$ at $L \in [40, 80]$ to $\sim 2.2$--$2.3$ at
$L \in [160, 200]$. Assuming $\gamma = 2.4$ everywhere overstates linear contrast in
darker channels by $\sim 1.5\times$ and generates a spurious negative additive offset
in photometric regressions.

We model each channel's forward response $v \in [0, 255] \mapsto I(v) \in [0, 1]$ with a
two-parameter toe-power law normalized to $I(0)=0$, $I(1)=1$:
\begin{equation}
  I_c(v) = \frac{\bigl(\frac{v/255 + a_c}{1 + a_c}\bigr)^{\gamma_c} - c_{0,c}}{1 - c_{0,c}},
  \qquad c_{0,c} = \left(\frac{a_c}{1 + a_c}\right)^{\gamma_c},
  \label{eq:crf}
\end{equation}
fitted nightly on \code{akdeniz} by Huber-robust minimization of
$\ln\bigl[(I_c(L_1)/I_c(L_0)) / (E_1/E_0)\bigr]$ over clean bracket pairs from the last
14~days. On November~2026 data, this simple two-parameter CRF cuts median
exposure-ratio error roughly $3\times$ in blue (\SI{4.46}{\percent} $\to$
\SI{1.74}{\percent}, $a_B = 0.678$, $\gamma_B = 4.19$) and green (\SI{5.39}{\percent}
$\to$ \SI{2.33}{\percent}, $a_G = 0.462$, $\gamma_G = 3.90$), and from
\SI{13.98}{\percent} to \SI{8.50}{\percent} in red ($a_R = 0.420$, $\gamma_R = 3.39$;
the residual red spread reflects the ISP's colour-correction matrix on saturated blue
sky). Linearizing with Eq.~\eqref{eq:crf} renders the blue and green dip essentially
exposure-invariant across $L \in [20, 248]$ and retires the legacy \code{scurve}
entirely.

\subsection{Bayesian Stan M2 scattering model (deployed nightly)}
\label{sec:bayes}

In linear radiance per channel $c \in \{B, G, R\}$, a defocused window contaminant both
attenuates background sky radiance $\Lbg$ and scatters ambient window illumination
$E_{\mathrm{win}}$ toward the sensor \citep{gu2009dirty}:
\begin{equation}
  \Lsp = t_c\,\Lbg + k_{\mathrm{fac},c}\,\Lfac + s_{0,c},
  \label{eq:m2}
\end{equation}
where we proxy direct-sun and courtyard illumination on the glass by the simultaneous
CRF-linearized radiance $\Lfac$ of a fixed $100\times 100$ patch on the sunlit
courtyard building facade (\code{spotlab.py}). Dividing by $\Lbg$ yields a dimensionless
effective transmittance $y_n = \Lsp/\Lbg$ with clean HMC geometry:
\begin{equation}
  y_n \;\sim\; \operatorname{Student-}t\!\left(\nu_{c(n)},\;
    t_{c(n)} + k_{\mathrm{fac},c(n)}\,\frac{L_{\mathrm{fac},n}}{L_{\mathrm{bg},n}},\;
    \sqrt{\sigma_n^2 + \sigma_{x,c(n)}^2}\right),
  \label{eq:stan}
\end{equation}
with weakly informative priors $1 - t_c \sim \mathrm{LogNormal}(\ln 0.05, 0.5)$,
$k_{\mathrm{fac},c} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.02)$,
$\nu_c \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(2, 0.15)$ bounded to $[2, 50]$ (preventing infinite-$\nu$
HMC rejections observed in the legacy script), and
$\sigma_{x,c} \sim \mathcal{N}_+(0, 0.015)$ bounded below at $10^{-4}$ (the 8-bit
quantization floor). The Student-$t$ likelihood guards against residual cloud edges
\citep{lange1989robust}.

In v11, Stan sampling ran on \code{pandr} (\code{spotfit.R}) as a disconnected record
under inverse-sRGB and found a troubling \emph{negative} constant offset $s_0 < 0$ in
all channels, which v11 carefully hedged as likely response-curve-confounded. Fitting
Eq.~\eqref{eq:m2} with $s_0$ included under our empirical CRF \textbf{vindicates that
hedge}: $s_{0,c}$ collapses statistically to zero in all three channels
(G: $+0.0013\,[-0.0089, +0.0108]$; R: $+0.0004\,[-0.0101, +0.0116]$;
B: $-0.0102\,[-0.0251, +0.0057]$), and is therefore dropped from the production model
Eq.~\eqref{eq:stan}.

Equation~\eqref{eq:stan} now runs nightly on \code{akdeniz} via \code{cmdstanpy}
\citep{carpenter2017stan} (4 parallel NUTS chains, \code{adapt\_delta=0.95},
$N \le 2000$--$3000$ clean observations from the trailing 7~days, $\sim$\SI{45}{s}).
Table~\ref{tab:stan} and Figure~\ref{fig:crf}b report the 2026-11-24 production
posterior ($N = 2921$, zero divergences, $\hat{R}_{\max} = 1.0055$, all diagnostics
passed):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Pure optical transmittance is achromatic:} $t_B = 0.9525$,
  $t_G = 0.9526$, $t_R = 0.9474$ (area-averaged over the $r < \SI{60}{px}$ photometry
  disk; $B$ and $G$ agree to \SI{0.01}{\percent}).
\item \textbf{Direct-sun forward scattering is detected at $>12\sigma$ in red:}
  $k_{\mathrm{fac},R} = +0.00724$ (90\% CI $[+0.00665, +0.00783]$; green $+0.00352$,
  blue $+0.00415$). When direct sunlight strikes the glass and facade against a dark
  red component of clear blue sky ($\Lfac/\Lbg \approx 3$--$4$), warm forward-scattered
  light partially refills the red shadow by $\sim\SIrange{2}{3}{\percent}$; on overcast
  or low-sun frames ($\Lfac/\Lbg \lesssim 0.3$), the scatter vanishes and the full
  optical dip appears.
\item \textbf{Unexplained scatter collapses $6\times$:} $\sigma_{x}$ falls from
  $0.5$--\SI{0.7}{\percent} under inverse-sRGB to \SI{0.07}{\percent} (B) and
  \SI{0.06}{\percent} (G), and $\nu$ rises from $\sim 3$ (heavy-tailed
  misspecification) to $13$--$32$ (near-Gaussian).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Position tracking, offline spatial validation, and honest epochs}
\label{sec:filter}

Two spatial position estimators exist. The \emph{production} nightly estimator
(\code{spotnight.py} on \code{akdeniz}) is deliberately fast and nonparametric: it
selects bright-then-flat frames from each bracket class (\code{br0}, \code{br1},
\code{br2}) over an adaptive 2-to-4-day window (widening automatically during overcast
spells), masks the top 20~ISP edge rows (\code{TOP\_MASK = 20}), divides each frame by
a sigma-clipped ridge-regularized background fit, normalizes each bracket's median dip
by its own peak (pinning position across brackets, which cut per-bracket disagreement
from $\sim\SI{60}{px}$ to $\sim\SIrange{2}{6}{px}$), and takes a half-depth centroid.
A recursive filter \citep{kalman1960,sarkka2023filtering} propagates last night's state
using a linear velocity fitted across the trailing 10 accepted nights (suppressing
one-night differencing noise once velocity fell below \SI{1}{px/day}), rejecting steps
$> \SI{45}{px}$ or bracket spreads $> \SI{18}{px}$ in favour of carry-forward.

In July, this cheap estimator was independently validated offline against a
hierarchical pixelwise Student-$t$ elliptical-blob model fitted in CmdStan via
multi-start L-BFGS \citep{liu1989lbfgs,papaspiliopoulos2007parametrization,betancourt2015hmc,gelman2020workflow}
(\code{spot\_paper/code/spothier.stan}), with positions linked across days by a
non-centred random walk and amplitude free per exposure bracket. The two estimators
agreed to $\sim\SI{3}{px}$ along the entire 8-day July track while sharing no machinery.

One bookkeeping discipline remains foundational: \textbf{every estimate carries its
data's mean epoch, never its run date}. Because the tracker stacks frames from the
preceding 2--4~days, its effective epoch trails the run by $\sim 1.5$~days; velocity is
computed against elapsed epoch time (px/day), and the publisher stamps \code{fit\_epoch}
into the bundle so downstream extrapolation cannot drift by $v\,\Delta t$ and print a
bright/dark dipole.

\section{The multi-bracket empirical transmittance map}
\label{sec:map}

The shadow's radial profile is distinctly flat-topped with compact support, falling far
faster than a Gaussian (Figure~\ref{fig:profile}a). The spatial centroid therefore only
\emph{locates}; correction shape comes from a registered per-channel empirical
transmittance map \citep{howell2006handbook},
\begin{equation}
  T_c(\mathbf{r}) \;=\; \operatorname{median}_j\,
     \frac{I_{j,c}(\mathbf{r})}{B_{j,c}(\mathbf{r})},
\end{equation}
stacked in CRF linear radiance with velocity registration (each frame's window shifted
to its interpolated centre so motion never smears the stack) and light Gaussian
denoising ($\sigma = \SI{1.5}{px}$).

Four months of operation forced three structural upgrades over v11:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Multi-bracket stacking (retiring fixed-exposure \code{ref} stacks).}
  Through November~2026, \code{publish\_live.py} stacked only fixed-exposure reference
  frames (\code{flatfield\_acc/}). In late-November winter daylight, $\sim\SI{70}{\percent}$
  of 24~h is dark and $\sim\SI{26}{\percent}$ of clear midday reference frames saturate
  the blue channel ($L > 249$), compounded by a subsampling bug
  (\code{fs[::max(1, len(fs)//400)][:400]} silently dropped the newest 337 frames when
  $n = 737$). On 2026-11-24 the legacy publisher failed outright (\code{only 13 usable
  frames/channel}) and previously baked a shallow midday-biased red map
  (\SI{3.25}{\percent} core vs.\ \SIrange{5}{9}{\percent} on overcast/morning frames).
  Stacking clean, unclipped frames across \emph{all three exposure brackets}
  (\code{br0/br1/br2}, where \code{br0} stays unclipped in clear blue sky and
  \code{br1/br2} stay bright under overcast), uniformly sampled across an adaptive
  3-to-6-day window with an encoded-DN flatness gate ($\mathrm{MAD} < 2.5$~DN), raised
  clean counts on the very same day from 13 to $[81, 98, 68]$ frames per channel in
  \SI{4}{s} on 8 threads.
\item \textbf{Top-edge ridge regularization and \code{TOP\_MASK = 20}.} At $y \approx 68$,
  the entire upper half of the legacy $r \in [105, 130]$~px annulus lies off the sensor.
  Fitting an unregularized 2D quadratic to a one-sided bottom arc makes the $y^2$ term
  ill-conditioned, wildly extrapolating cloud edges up to $y = 68$ (even producing
  apparent negative dips when the core was 19~DN darker than the ring). Tightening the
  ring to $r \in [85, 115]$~px (where Figure~\ref{fig:profile}a shows deficit is already
  $< \SI{0.06}{\percent}$), masking $y < 20$, and adding a ridge penalty
  $\mathrm{diag}(0, 0, 0, 50, 50, 200)$ on the quadratic terms whenever $y < r_1 + 20$
  completely stabilizes edge extrapolation.
\item \textbf{Clean-sky selection independent of dip.} Confirming the lucky-imaging
  selection-bias caveat \citep{law2006lucky,smith2009lucky}, selecting on ring
  flatness and unclipped brightness outside the blemish avoids cloud contamination
  without conditioning on the core.
\end{itemize}

\section{Live Bayesian MAP correction}
\label{sec:apply}

Every 5~minutes on \code{pandr}, \code{flatfield.py} corrects the three raw HDR
brackets in CRF linear radiance before Mertens fusion:

\paragraph{1. Placement (prediction + symmetric lock).}
The bundle's published position is extrapolated from \code{fit\_epoch} by velocity
(clamped to $[-0.5, 3.5]$~days and $\le \SI{40}{px}$). On daylight HDR calls
(median $> 30$~DN), a single template lock \citep{lewis1995ncc} runs on the brightest
unclipped bracket using a \emph{symmetric} vertical search bound
$r_y = \min(25, \max(0, y - 48))$ so top-edge clamping at $y = 68$ never introduces a
one-sided downward bias; decisive locks ($\mathrm{NCC} \ge 0.50$, rival-peak margin
$\ge 0.08$) are smoothed over a trailing 1-hour window. Single-frame auxiliary calls
(\code{sky\_ref}, \code{science.py}) apply the correction without writing duplicate
lock or provenance entries.

\paragraph{2. Conjugate Bayesian MAP depth across brackets.}
From the placed map $T_c$, we extract the normalized 2D spatial shape
$p_c(\mathbf{r}) = \mathrm{clip}(1 - T_c(\mathbf{r}), 0, 0.25)\,w(\mathbf{r}) / d_{0,c}$
(feathered smoothly to zero outside $r = \SI{82}{px}$ and normalized so
$\mathrm{median}_{\mathrm{core}}(p_c) = 1$). Because the blemish's optical shape
$p_c$ is fixed while its effective depth $d_c$ varies with sunlight scattering
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:stan}), each channel $c$ is solved in closed form:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Physical prior from Stan~M2:} reading the sunlit facade patch $\Lfac$ on
  the current frame gives
  $d_{\mathrm{prior},c} = s_{\mathrm{core},c}\bigl((1 - t_c) - k_{\mathrm{fac},c}\,\Lfac/\Lbg\bigr)$,
  with prior variance $\tau_c^2 = \max(3\,s_{\mathrm{core},c}\,\sigma_{x,c},\; 0.022)^2$.
  Even under solid cloud, the prior already accounts for direct-sun scattering.
\item \textbf{Matched-filter likelihood across unclipped brackets:} on every unclipped
  bracket $i$ with ring roughness $\rho_i = \mathrm{MAD}_i/\mathrm{median}(B_i) < 0.08$,
  the optimal linear projection of the observed deficit onto $p_c$ is
  $\hat d_{i,c} = \langle p_c, 1 - I_{i,c}/B_{i,c}\rangle / \langle p_c, p_c\rangle$,
  with variance $\sigma_{i,c}^2 = (0.40\,\rho_i)^2 + 0.003^2$.
\item \textbf{Conjugate posterior and clipped-bracket inheritance:} pooling across
  unclipped brackets yields
  $d_{\mathrm{pool},c} = \bigl(d_{\mathrm{prior},c}/\tau_c^2 + \sum_i \hat d_{i,c}/\sigma_{i,c}^2\bigr) / \bigl(1/\tau_c^2 + \sum_i 1/\sigma_{i,c}^2\bigr)$,
  clamped safely to $[-0.015, 0.14]$ (allowing slight bright-scatter fill-in without
  runaway gain; applied transmittance clamped to $[0.84, 1.02]$). Crucially,
  \emph{partially clipped brackets} (e.g.\ \code{br1} where clean sky clips at 255 while
  the darkened core sits at 241) inherit $d_{\mathrm{pool},c}$ from the unclipped
  brackets instead of being skipped or zero-trimmed. For low-level red ($L < 120$),
  where the ISP colour matrix introduces mild bracket-specific curvature, each clean
  bracket shrinks gently toward its own $\hat d_{i,R}$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Two subtle failure modes uncovered in production.}
First, \textbf{unsigned-integer truncation}: every version of \code{flatfield.py} from
July through early November cast float outputs via
\code{np.clip(o, 0, 255).astype(np.uint8)} rather than rounding with \code{np.rint()}.
Floating-point round-trip values like $179.9999$ floored to $179$, darkening every
corrected pixel by $0.5$--$1.0$~DN ($\sim\SIrange{0.7}{1.5}{\percent}$ in linear light,
$\sim\SIrange{2}{3}{\percent}$ after CLAHE). Adding \code{np.rint()} immediately cut
synthetic ground-truth residuals from \SI{+1.50}{\percent} to
\SIrange{+0.22}{+0.36}{\percent} and live Mertens residuals from \SI{+0.51}{\percent}
to \SI{-0.12}{\percent}.

Second, \textbf{CLAHE tile-seam measurement bias at the top edge}: on \SI{4}{K} frames,
8$\times$8 CLAHE places a vertical tile boundary at $x = 1440$ (right beside
$x = 1481$) and transitions from clamped border tile~0 to bilinear row interpolation at
$y = 135$ (splitting our $y = 68$ core from the bottom ring at $y \approx 173$--$198$).
Replacing the spot in all three raw brackets with exact smooth quadratic sky (0.00\%
optical defect) still produced a \textbf{\SIrange{+3.0}{+3.9}{\percent} phantom
post-CLAHE dip} under v11's one-sided display metric. Ongoing verification
(\code{spot\_ab.py}) therefore reports \textbf{pre-CLAHE Mertens linear dips per
channel} alongside enhanced display dips.

Safety remains strictly \emph{model-referenced} (no scene-referenced cap that eats
bright clouds). In the historical July archive reprocessing (\code{backfill.py}),
display-space closed-loop gains (median $2.83$, compensating CLAHE's $\sim 2.85\times$
amplification; distinct from any linear-space model clamp) are reported separately as
an offline oracle upper bound.

\section{Simplified two-tier architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

The November~2026 refactor collapsed four overlapping scripts across two hosts
(\code{spotnight.py}, \code{publish\_live.py}, \code{refiner.py}, and \code{pandr}'s
unused \code{spotfit.R}) into \textbf{one unified nightly pipeline}

\label{sec:results}

\section{Physical identification, mount creep, and corrected optics}
\label{sec:physics}

\subsection{Exact pupil-shadow solve and off-axis ellipticity}

For a camera of entrance-pupil diameter $a \approx \SI{1.08}{mm}$ focused at nominal
distance $F \approx \SI{0.5}{m}$, the ray bundle collected by a single sensor pixel
crosses a window at distance $d$ from the entrance pupil as a circular patch of diameter
$a' = a(1 - d/F)$ \citep{goodman2017fourier,potmesil1981lens,willson2005dust}. An
opaque speck of diameter $s < a'$ convolved with that circular pupil produces a
\emph{flat-topped} shadow whose central fractional attenuation is $(s/a')^2$ and whose
\textbf{exact half-maximum diameter equals the projected pupil diameter $a'$ itself}
(to $O((s/a')^2)$), not $a' + s$.

In v11, a subtle inconsistency crept in: the text plugged the Gaussian-fit FWHM
(\SI{76}{px}) into $(s + a')/d$ to infer $d \approx \SIrange{30}{35}{mm}$, while
reporting an empirical half-depth radius of $r_{1/2} \approx \SI{50.5}{px}$
($\mathrm{FWHM} \approx \SI{101}{px}$) 40~lines later — and then had to posit an
entrance pupil \SI{2}{cm} behind the front glass of a \SI{4.3}{mm} wide-angle lens.
That pupil depth is geometrically impossible for a compact \ang{90}-FOV lens (a pupil
\SI{20}{mm} back at $\pm\ang{45}$ requires a $> \SI{40}{mm}$ front element to avoid
vignetting, and retrofocus designs place the entrance pupil \emph{near} the front
element, not deep behind it).

Using the consistent empirical flat-top width $\mathrm{FWHM} = \SI{101}{px}$
($r_{1/2} = 51.3 / 50.3 / 48.9\text{ px}$ in B/G/R, unchanged to \SI{0.4}{px} over four
months; Figure~\ref{fig:profile}a) resolves the contradiction immediately. With
paraxial focal length $f_{\mathrm{px}} = \sqrt{3840^2 + 2160^2}/2 \approx \SI{2203}{px}$,
at the blemish's off-axis angle $\theta = \arctan(1103 / 2203) = \ang{26.6}$
($\sec\theta = 1.118$, geometric-mean area stretch $\sec^{3/2}\theta = 1.182$):
\begin{equation}
  d \;\approx\; \frac{g_{\theta}\,f_{\mathrm{px}}\,a}{\mathrm{FWHM}_{\mathrm{px}} + g_{\theta}\,f_{\mathrm{px}}\,a/F}
    \;\approx\; \SIrange{22.6}{26.5}{mm} \quad (g_\theta \in [1, 1.182]),
\end{equation}
yielding $d \approx \SIrange{23}{28}{mm}$ — directly consistent with $\sim\SIrange{4}{6}{mm}$
pupil inset behind the front element plus camera-to-pane gap and glass/double-pane
thickness (if the speck sits on the outer surface of Porter Hall's insulated glazing).
The corrected area ratio $(s/a')^2 \approx 0.052$ with $a' \approx \SI{1.02}{mm}$ gives
a speck diameter of $s \approx \SIrange{0.23}{0.24}{mm}$, and a translational lever of
$f_{\mathrm{px}}/d \approx \SIrange{78}{97}{px/mm}$ on axis ($\sim\SIrange{95}{115}{px/mm}$
off axis).

An independent geometric prediction confirms this rectilinear model: an off-axis
circular pupil shadow at angle $\theta$ projects onto a flat sensor as an ellipse
stretched radially by $\sec^2\theta$ and tangentially by $\sec\theta$, giving an axis
ratio of $\sec\theta = 1.118$ pointed directly toward the optical axis $(1920, 1080)$
(radial angle \ang{66.4}). On the November~24 \code{TMAP} (Figure~\ref{fig:profile}b),
the weighted second-moment ellipse has axis ratio \textbf{1.095} oriented at
\textbf{\ang{57.7}} — matching both magnitude and orientation to within \ang{8.7}.

\subsection{125-day viscoelastic mount creep: an unintended strain gauge}

In July (8~days of data), v11 attributed the blemish's \SI{120}{px} walk to reversible
thermal breathing of a sun-struck window sash. Four months of tracking (73~accepted
nightly fixes spanning 125~days, 2026-07-22 to 2026-11-24; Figure~\ref{fig:creep})
falsify reversible thermal expansion and reveal something much cleaner:
\begin{itemize}
\item The shadow moved monotonically along a single straight line from $(1188.7, 172.4)$
  to $(1480.8, 68.0)$: $\Delta x = +292.2$~px, $\Delta y = -104.4$~px, total distance
  \SI{310.3}{px}, with an RMS perpendicular deviation of just \textbf{\SI{4.1}{px}}
  (1.3\% of path length) and \emph{zero} seasonal reversal as Pittsburgh cooled from
  July heat to November winter.
\item Its speed decayed smoothly by nearly two orders of magnitude: $\sim\SI{14}{px/day}$
  in late July $\to \SI{3.0}{px/day}$ in August $\to \SI{1.7}{px/day}$ in September
  $\to \SI{0.8}{px/day}$ in October $\to <\SI{0.3}{px/day}$ in late November.
\item Along-axis displacement $s(t)$ is poorly fit by constant-velocity drift
  ($R^2 = 0.873$, $\mathrm{RMSE} = \SI{25.9}{px}$), but follows \textbf{Andrade's
  universal $t^{1/3}$ primary viscoelastic creep law} \citep{andrade1910creep},
  $s(t) = A\,t^{\alpha}$ with $\alpha = \mathbf{0.339}$ ($R^2 = \mathbf{0.9889}$,
  $\mathrm{RMSE} = \SI{7.6}{px}$), and logarithmic relaxation $A\ln(1 + t/\tau)$
  ($\tau = \SI{2.19}{d}$, $R^2 = 0.9854$, $\mathrm{RMSE} = \SI{8.8}{px}$).
\end{itemize}
At $\sim\SI{95}{px/mm}$, the entire 125-day walk corresponds to $\sim\SIrange{3.1}{3.7}{mm}$
of monotonic mechanical settling of the camera mount after its July~17 installation.
The accidental dust speck functions as a \SI{10}{\micro\meter}-resolution optical
strain gauge invisible in the distant skyline.

\subsection{Why v11's extinction-paradox and $\lambda^{-2}$ arguments were wrong}
\label{sec:correction}

In the interest of scientific self-correction, we explicitly retract two optical claims
made in v11 (\S\S9--10 of the July draft) and verified algebraically in
\code{lean\_verification/SpotFit/Section10.lean}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Extended sky illumination obeys reciprocity, not collimated extinction.}
  Version~11 interpreted the $\sim 5\%$ core depth (near $1\times (s/a)^2$ rather than
  $2\times (s/a)^2$) as an instantiation of wide-acceptance recollection of the
  extinction paradox \citep{vandehulst1981light,bohren1983absorption,berg2011extinction,deepak1978forward,lai2004beam}
  and cited \citet{markel2020extinction}'s $\sim\ang{60}$ aperture criterion. Two
  errors undermine that framing. First, those theorems treat a particle illuminated by
  a \emph{collimated plane wave or narrow beam}; our camera views an \emph{incoherent,
  extended diffuse sky}. By Helmholtz reciprocity (conservation of radiance), elastic
  forward diffraction in front of a uniform sky cannot darken the image at \emph{any}
  pupil acceptance angle, because rays diffracted out of a pixel's cone are exactly
  compensated by rays diffracted in from adjacent sky directions. Second, the direction
  of Markel's $\sim\ang{60}$ comparison was inverted: measuring far-field extinction
  ($Q_{\mathrm{ext}} = 2$) requires a \emph{tiny} acceptance angle ($\ll \lambda/s$),
  whereas a large $\sim\ang{60}$ integrating aperture collects forward scatter and
  converges toward \emph{absorption}.
\item \textbf{The $\lambda^{-2}$ recollection law is empirically refuted by CRF
  calibration.} Version~11 calibrated a $\rho(\lambda) \propto \lambda^{-2}$ texture
  recollection model to July's uncalibrated inverse-sRGB map integrals
  (B:G:R $= 1.00 : 1.08 : 1.07$). Under our empirical CRF in November, raw daytime
  integrated deficits \emph{invert} to $1.000 : 1.097 : 0.904$ (red dropping 10\% below
  blue because winter sun elevates the red $k_{\mathrm{fac}}\Lfac/\Lbg$ forward-scatter
  term!), and once $k_{\mathrm{fac}}$ is factored out in Stan~M2, pure optical blocking
  $1 - t_c$ is achromatic in B and G (\SI{4.75}{\percent} vs.\ \SI{4.74}{\percent};
  Table~\ref{tab:stan}). What \emph{does} persist reproducibly across four months is a
  \SI{2.4}{px} (5\%) width ordering ($r_{1/2}$: B \SI{51.3}{px}, G \SI{50.3}{px},
  R \SI{48.9}{px}), which is consistent with modest axial/pupil chromatic aberration or
  ISP chroma processing rather than $\lambda^{-2}$ diffraction recollection.
\end{enumerate}

The net scientific outcome is stronger, not weaker: replacing a tenuous far-field
diffraction analogy with empirical CRF calibration directly confirmed the
\citet{gu2009dirty} dirty-lens scattering equation (Eq.~\eqref{eq:m2}) at $>12\sigma$
for a single grain, eliminated the spurious negative offset, and cut live residuals by
an order of magnitude.

\section{Relation to prior work}
\label{sec:literature}

The artifact-removal literature separates cleanly into static physics-based models for
lens or sensor dust \citep{willson2005dust,zhou2007dust,gu2007dirtyglass,gu2009dirty},
multi-view parallax methods requiring camera motion \citep{xue2015obstruction},
refractive raindrop models \citep{you2016adherent}, data-driven automotive soiling
segmentation/inpainting \citep{uricar2019soiling}, and population-level dust deposition
censuses on Mars \citep{chenchen2023insight}. This work contributes three pieces that
sit at the intersection:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Single-grain validation of \citet{gu2009dirty} with explicit scatter
  forcing:} measuring both achromatic attenuation ($t \approx 0.952$) and direct-sun
  additive scattering ($k_{\mathrm{fac},R} = +0.0072$) per channel in a consumer HDR
  camera without laboratory calibration.
\item \textbf{Continuous 125-day tracking and mount metrology:} showing that even a
  rigidly fixed camera violates the calibrate-once assumption by $>3\times$ the
  shadow's FWHM through viscoelastic mount creep ($\alpha = 0.34$), while turning the
  defocused shadow into a passive sub-millimetre strain gauge.
\item \textbf{Closed-loop Bayesian deployment with honest prospective auditing:}
  demonstrating how long-running autonomous correction actually fails in the wild
  (winter clipping, integer truncation, edge-geometry conditioning, CLAHE measurement
  artifacts) and how hierarchical shrinkage toward a live physical Stan posterior fixes
  those failures without black-box inpainting.
\end{itemize}

\par}

\paragraph{Remaining limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Post-upgrade prospective window is one day so far:} while the legacy
  pipeline has \num{2450} prospective A/B triples over 95~days, the \code{bayes3}
  upgrade was deployed on 2026-11-24 ($n=18$ clean live triples verified); multi-week
  winter accumulation in \code{ab\_log.csv} is ongoing.
\item \textbf{Uncalibrated local plate scale and wipe test:} $d \approx \SIrange{23}{28}{mm}$
  and the $\sim\SI{95}{px/mm}$ lever inherit $\sim 20\%$ geometric uncertainty until a
  controlled camera translation or physical wipe test (inner vs.\ outer pane) is
  performed.
\item \textbf{Consumer ISP red-channel nonlinearity:} even after two-parameter CRF
  calibration, red exposure ratios retain \SI{8.5}{\percent} median error from the ISP
  colour matrix on saturated blue sky; raw Bayer readout would eliminate this entirely.
\item \textbf{Top-edge margin:} at $y \approx 68$~px, the upward template-lock range is
  already narrowed to \SI{20}{px}; if mount creep were to resume upward past $y < 55$,
  physically lifting the camera a few millimetres remains the only permanent remedy.
\item \textbf{Extreme distractors:} no systematic false-correction stress test has been
  run on birds, contrails, or direct solar glare sitting squarely inside the footprint,
  though the Bayesian roughness shrinkage and model-referenced depth bounds
  ($[-0.015, 0.14]$) strictly cap any perturbation.
\end{itemize}
