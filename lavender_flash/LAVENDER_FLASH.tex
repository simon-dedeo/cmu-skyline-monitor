\begin{abstract}
\noindent
At 00:11~EDT on 9 August 2026, a fixed-exposure skyline camera in Pittsburgh
recorded a single 100~ms frame in which an overcast midnight sky turned pale
lavender: $(R,G,B)=(172,170,223)$ against a sodium-amber baseline of
$(22,19,7)$. Treating this capture as a colorimetric closure test, we
propagate a three-component lightning spectrum---continuum, N\,\textsc{ii}
and hydrogen Balmer lines, and \Ntp{} first-negative bands---through cloud,
air, and an archive-validated daylight-balanced camera model. Three physical
constraints follow: an optically thin Kramers continuum is strongly favored
over a 30\,000~K blackbody; H$\alpha$ (656.3~nm) from dissociated water vapor
is strictly required to lift $R/G>1$; and molecular \Ntp{} is bounded to
$0$--$14\%$ of visible energy, consistent with return-stroke spectroscopy.
We connect this nitrogen spectrum to lightning's abiotic fixation pathways
and close with the metameric color theory of a pale violet no eye witnessed.
\end{abstract}

\section{The observation and 4K radiometry}
\label{sec:obs}
The monitor captures a radiometric reference frame of the Carnegie Mellon
skyline roughly every 5.4~minutes ($\sim$326~s cadence) with
sun-elevation-keyed manual exposure and gain and fixed 5600~K daylight white
balance. On the night of 8--9 August 2026 the log (\code{science.csv}, row
logged at 00:12:15~EDT immediately after the 00:11~EDT reference capture) shows
one anomalous sample: full-frame brightness 138.7 (neighbors 23.1--31.4) and
central-sky swatch \code{\#b9b5ed}. The 100~ms exposure integrated one or more
strokes of a flash whose light, diffused by the overcast deck, illuminated the
scene to twilight levels (Fig.~\ref{fig:frames}). Two later samples (00:33 and
00:39~EDT; sky swatches \code{\#11151d} and \code{\#111722}) show weak blue
excursions consistent with more distant flashes.

We extract the flash radiometry directly from the archived 4K
($3840\times 2160$) reference triplet. While the logged swatch \code{\#b9b5ed}
is the OKLab perceptual average over the monitor's central sky box (rows
5--35\%, middle third) covering the brightest cloud region, the full-width
upper quarter (top 25\%) gives raw mean $(R,G,B)=(172.0,169.8,223.1)$
(\code{\#acaadf}) against $(21.9,18.6,7.0)$ at 00:06~EDT and $(9.3,8.4,4.0)$
at 00:17~EDT. (Because the night exposure schedule interpolates gain slowly
with solar depression, \code{ref\_gain} stepped from 86 to 87 between 00:06
and 00:11 and remained 87 at 00:17.) Linearizing pixel-by-pixel with the
standard sRGB transfer function \citep{iec1999} and subtracting the mean of
the bracketing baselines yields the flash-only linear sky color
\begin{equation}
  (r,g,b)_{\rm flash} = (0.414,\,0.403,\,0.747), \qquad
  R/G = 1.028,\quad B/G = 1.854 .
  \label{eq:meas}
\end{equation}
Because the flash is $\sim$40--100$\times$ brighter than the sodium-amber
baseline in linear radiance, subtracting only the `before' frame
($R/G=1.026$, $B/G=1.863$) or only the gain-matched `after' frame
($R/G=1.030$, $B/G=1.845$) shifts the ratios by less than $1\%$.

Binning the top-25\% sky pixels by flash blue digital number ($B_{\rm DN}$)
characterizes the sensor's nonlinear response directly inside the flash frame
(Fig.~\ref{fig:radiometry}). First, under standard sRGB linearization, $R/G$
drifts upward by $+6.3\%$ from $0.982$ at $B_{\rm DN}\approx 182$ to $1.044$
at $B_{\rm DN}\approx 245$. Applying the empirical per-channel Camera Response
Function (CRF) independently calibrated for this Elgato Facecam~4K from
daytime exposure brackets in the monitor's flat-field pipeline
($L_c(x)\propto [(x+a_c)/(1+a_c)]^{\gamma_c}-c_{0,c}$ with
$(a,\gamma)_R=(0.36,3.2)$, $(0.45,3.8)_G$, $(0.55,3.8)_B$, anchored neutral at
DN~180) flattens that drift to $R/G = 1.026$--$1.037$ across the entire
unclipped sky (and produces the same flattening on daytime overcast skies in
\code{science.csv}), yielding top-25\% CRF ratios $R/G=1.030$, $B/G=1.905$.
Second, above $B_{\rm DN}\approx 220$, $B/G$ rolls off monotonically under
both sRGB ($1.919\to 1.754$) and the empirical CRF ($1.975\to 1.794$), driven
by ISP highlight-shoulder compression and partial blue clipping ($1.04\%$ of
top-25\% pixels reach $B\ge 254$, rising to $11.6\%$ in the top-5\% central
sky). Restricting to the unclipped, below-shoulder sky ($170\le B_{\rm DN}<215$)
gives $R/G=0.997$, $B/G=1.914$ (sRGB) or $R/G=1.027$, $B/G=1.969$ (CRF). Thus
the true flash-only ratios lie tightly in $R/G\approx 1.00$--$1.03$ and
$B/G\approx 1.85$--$1.97$.

Finally, because the Elgato Facecam~4K uses a CMOS rolling shutter (readout
time $\sim$17--33~ms at 4K), a millisecond-scale flash could in principle
illuminate only a horizontal band of rows. Computing the full-frame vertical
profile of $(I_{\rm flash}-I_{\rm before})$ across all 2160~rows shows smooth,
continuous illumination from the top cloud deck through the building
fa\c{c}ades down to the foreground lawn with no exposure-cutoff edge,
confirming that the flash occurred during the $\sim$70--80~ms
simultaneous-integration window shared by all rows. Peak sky brightness sits
near the top edge slightly right of center (columns 50--67\%, camera pointing
roughly north), placing the flash azimuth toward the north-northeast above the
horizon.

\section{Physics: the anatomy of the emission}
\label{sec:physics}

\subsection{The channel}
A return stroke drives $\sim$30~kA through a centimeter-scale channel,
shock-heating it to a peak of 28\,000--31\,000~K within microseconds
\citep{prueitt1963,orville1968,rakovuman2003}, with high-speed triggered-lightning
spectroscopy resolving peak temperatures occasionally exceeding 35\,000--40\,000~K
in the initial sub-microsecond core \citep{walker2017,walker2019}. Electron
densities inferred from Stark broadening of H$\alpha$ start near
$10^{18}\,\mathrm{cm^{-3}}$ and fall by an order of magnitude within tens of
microseconds \citep{uman1964}; temperature falls through $\sim$20\,000~K on
the same timescale and reaches $\sim$10\,000~K by 50~$\mu$s \citep{orville1968}.
Crucially, \citet{uman1965} demonstrated that the channel is \emph{optically
thin} across the visible continuum and most N\,\textsc{ii} lines over nearly
the entire luminous phase, approaching optical thickness only briefly in the
initial microsecond core and in strong self-absorbed line cores (such as
H$\alpha$ and neutral UV/NIR multiplets). A camera exposure of 100~ms
integrates the entire thermodynamic history of every stroke it catches---and a
typical cloud-to-ground flash contains 3--5 strokes spread over 200--300~ms
\citep{rakovuman2003}---so the recorded color is a time- and
stroke-integrated mixture.

\subsection{The continuum}
Beneath the discrete lines lies a genuine continuum, produced in the
transparent plasma primarily by two free-electron processes: free--free
bremsstrahlung (electrons scattering in ion fields) and free--bound radiative
recombination (electrons captured predominantly onto N$^+$ and O$^+$, with
H$^+$ contributing in humid air) \citep{uman1965,zeldovich1966,orvillehenderson1984}.
Recombination continua carry series-edge structure (e.g.\ the hydrogen Balmer
edge at 364.6~nm outside the camera passband and the Paschen continuum across
the visible below 820~nm, alongside dense N and O recombination continua).
The spectral \emph{slope} depends strongly on opacity and temperature. In an
optically thick limit at 30\,000~K, the channel radiates a Planck curve, an
effective $\lambda^{-3.5}$ slope across 400--700~nm. Once optically thin,
free--free and hydrogenic free--bound emission between series edges follow the
much flatter Kramers form, $\varepsilon_\lambda \propto \lambda^{-2}
e^{-hc/\lambda kT}$: an effective $\lambda^{-1.1}$ at 30\,000~K, flattening
further to $\lambda^{-0.6}$ at 20\,000~K and $\lambda^{-0.3}$ at 15\,000~K as
the channel cools. Because the real continuum is optically thin over almost
all of its luminous lifetime \citep{uman1965}, the Kramers form at
20\,000--30\,000~K is the physically natural baseline, with the 30\,000~K
Planck curve serving as the extreme optically-thick blue limit.

\subsection{The atomic lines}
At channel temperatures ($kT \simeq 2.6$~eV) collisional excitation populates
highly excited levels in singly ionized nitrogen (and oxygen), so
N\,\textsc{ii} dominates the hot phase: multiplets at 399.5, 444.7, and
463--464~nm in the blue--violet, with prominent lines at 500.5~nm (near the
peak visible irradiance band of \cite{orvillehenderson1984}) and 568.0~nm
\citep{salanave1961,wallace1964,orville1968}. As the channel cools, ionized
lines fade and the hydrogen Balmer series takes over: H$\alpha$ at 656.3~nm
peaks tens of microseconds after peak current and persists longest
\citep{orville1968}. Hydrogen is not native to dry air---it is stripped from
dissociated water vapor, making H$\alpha$ conspicuously strong in humid air
\citep{orville1968,rakovuman2003}; surface relative humidity on the night of
August~9 sat at 81--85\% in the local weather feed (86--90\% in reanalysis).
The strongest neutral emissions (O\,\textsc{i} 777.4~nm and N\,\textsc{i}
740--870~nm multiplets) fall beyond the camera's IR-cut filter, leaving weak
O\,\textsc{i} 615.8~nm inside the passband. We represent the atomic component
with a 10-line core template ($\sigma=2.5$--3~nm Gaussian profiles standing in
for Stark-broadened multiplets, relative energies ordered per
\cite{orville1968,orvillehenderson1984}, H$\alpha$ strongest) and test
sensitivity against both an expanded 22-line literature template (adding
H$\gamma$, H$\delta$, and secondary blue N\,\textsc{ii}/O\,\textsc{ii}
multiplets) and an H$\alpha$-ablated template with 656.3~nm removed.

\subsection{The molecular bands}
\label{sec:molecular}
A third possible visible component is molecular: the \Ntp{} first-negative
system ($B^2\Sigma_u^+ \to X^2\Sigma_g^+$), with bandheads at 391.4, 427.8,
and 470.9~nm \citep{salanave1962,wallace1964,orville1968}. Exciting it
requires ionizing N$_2$ (15.6~eV) and lifting the ion a further $\sim$3.2~eV,
conditions found not in the fully dissociated return-stroke core---where
slitless and high-speed triggered-lightning spectrographs consistently find
\Ntp{} bands weak or absent \citep{salanave1962,walker2017,walker2019}---but
in cooler peripheral regions: the corona sheath, stepped leaders, streamers,
and intracloud discharges \citep{wallace1964,orville1968,rakovuman2003}.

\subsection{Archive camera validation and the colorimetric closure test}
\label{sec:fit}
Before inverting the flash color, we validate our camera model against
known-spectrum skies recorded by the exact same instrument under the exact
same locked 5600~K white balance. Extracting high-sun reference frames from
\code{science.csv} provides two daytime calibration anchors
(Table~\ref{tab:validation}):
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \emph{Overcast sky} ($n=481$, solar elevation $>45^\circ$, cloud cover
  $\ge 95\%$): median DN $(163,179,207)$, sRGB-linearized $R/G=0.845$
  (IQR $0.80$--$0.87$), $B/G=1.317$ (IQR $1.28$--$1.36$).
  \item \emph{Clear blue sky} ($n=288$, solar elevation $>50^\circ$, cloud
  cover $\le 5\%$): median DN $(93.5,134,189)$, sRGB-linearized $R/G=0.479$
  (IQR $0.44$--$0.61$), $B/G=2.189$ (IQR $1.79$--$2.30$).
\end{itemize}

Consumer Bayer filters overlap broadly across wavelength, so raw
white-balanced Bayer integrals are much less saturated than the camera's sRGB
output; every digital camera ISP applies a $3\times 3$ color-correction matrix
(CCM) with negative off-diagonal terms to map raw sensor RGB into linear sRGB
(approximating the CIE~1931 color-matching functions adapted from 5600~K to
D65). Table~\ref{tab:validation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:spectrum}b confirm this
quantitatively: un-matrixed Gaussian Bayer curves reach only $B/G=1.67$ on an
85\% Rayleigh clear blue sky ($1.81$ at 100\% Rayleigh), falling well short of
the measured clear-sky median of $2.19$. Adding a least-squares
white-point-preserving $3\times 3$ CCM to the same Bayer curves
(Bayer$+$CCM), or using the sensor-independent CIE-sRGB pipeline directly,
reproduces clear blue sky ($B/G=2.15$--$2.17$) and overcast sky ($B/G=1.16$ at
nominal 5600~K, rising to $1.26$--$1.34$ if effective WB sits near 5200~K or
overcast CCT near 7000~K).

We model the time- and stroke-integrated spectral energy distribution as
\begin{equation}
  S(\lambda) \;=\; w_c\,\hat{C}(\lambda)
  \;+\; w_a\,\hat{L}_{\rm atomic}(\lambda)
  \;+\; w_m\,\hat{L}_{\mathrm{N}_2^+}(\lambda),
  \qquad w_c+w_a+w_m=1,
\end{equation}
with each template normalized to unit visible energy over 380--720~nm and
propagated through neutral cloud diffusion plus clear-air Rayleigh extinction
(\S\ref{sec:engineering}). For any fixed set of three templates, the
normalization constraint leaves \emph{two} free weights against \emph{two}
measured chromatic ratios---zero degrees of freedom, a unique linear solve
rather than an over-determined fit. The closure test retains genuine
falsification power because the non-negative simplex ($w_i\ge 0$) occupies
only a small wedge of the $(R/G,B/G)$ plane (Fig.~\ref{fig:spectrum}b).

Under the validated CIE-sRGB (5600~K) pipeline with an optically thin
30\,000~K Kramers continuum and the core atomic template, the exact solve is
\begin{equation}
  (w_c,\,w_a,\,w_m) = (0.57,\,0.38,\,0.05)
  \;\;\longrightarrow\;\;
  R/G = 1.028,\quad B/G = 1.854,
\end{equation}
reproducing the observed sky band to within 1~DN: modeled $(173,171,224)$
versus measured $(172,170,223)$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:spectrum}a). Three structural
conclusions follow across our sensitivity checks:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
  \item \emph{Optically thin (and/or cooling) continuum is favored over
  30\,000~K Planck.} Under CIE-sRGB and Bayer$+$CCM at 5600~K (3~km Rayleigh),
  both a 30\,000~K Planck continuum ($B/G=2.10$) and the atomic line template
  ($B/G=2.22$--$2.62$) lie \emph{above} the measured flash ($B/G=1.85$--$1.97$;
  Fig.~\ref{fig:spectrum}b). No non-negative mixture of 30\,000~K Planck,
  atomic lines, and \Ntp{} can lower $B/G$ to the measurement unless either
  the continuum is optically thin Kramers ($B/G=1.13$--$1.25$ at
  20\,000--30\,000~K), as established spectroscopically by \citet{uman1965},
  or atmospheric path reddening is substantially stronger than 3~km (at 10~km
  Rayleigh path, 30\,000~K Planck drops to $B/G=1.84$--$1.86$).

  \item \emph{H$\alpha$ (656.3~nm) is strictly required.} Removing H$\alpha$
  from the atomic template collapses its red ratio from $R/G=1.24$ to $0.36$
  in CIE-sRGB (and $1.90\to 0.26$ in Bayer$+$CCM). Every hot continuum also
  sits below unity ($R/G=0.60$ Planck, $0.91$ Kramers 30\,000~K, $0.98$
  Kramers 20\,000~K), and \Ntp{} sits in the violet with negligible red
  energy. No non-negative mixture without H$\alpha$ can cross $R/G=1$ under
  \emph{any} camera pipeline (Fig.~\ref{fig:spectrum}b). The lavender hue is
  built from nitrogen's blue--violet ions (and hot continuum) at one end and
  humid-air hydrogen's red H$\alpha$ at the other, with the green channel left
  as the local minimum between them.

  \item \emph{Molecular \Ntp{} emission is bounded ($0$--$14\%$), not
  required.} In CIE-sRGB at 5600~K, the Kramers$+$atomic solve puts $w_m=0.05$
  ($0.07$ under empirical CRF linearization at $B/G=1.905$); Bayer$+$CCM with
  the core template gives $w_m=0.14$, whereas using the expanded 22-line
  atomic template or an overcast-calibrated 5200~K white balance drives $w_m$
  to $0$ (Table~\ref{tab:validation}). This agrees directly with slitless and
  high-speed triggered-lightning spectroscopy, where \Ntp{} is weak or absent
  in the bright return-stroke channel \citep{salanave1962,walker2017,walker2019}.
  (Omitting the CCM artificially depresses broadband blueness from $B/G\ge 2.1$
  down to $\le 1.64$, which would falsely mimic a large \Ntp{} band.)
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Breaking the remaining degeneracies}
\label{sec:degeneracy}
Although three frozen templates against two ratios yield zero formal degrees
of freedom, the physical inversion remains under-determined once continuum
temperature and opacity, atomic line ratios, extinction path length, and the
unit's exact CCM and IR-cut edge are allowed to vary. Several escapes are
available, in ascending order of effort:
\emph{(i)~Time-resolved priors.} Spectrophotometry constrains typical
return-stroke continuum and line ratios across stroke phases
\citep{orville1968,orvillehenderson1984,walker2019}.
\emph{(ii)~In-scene spectrophotometric calibration.} Beyond the daytime sky
archive and bracket-derived CRF used here, photographing a Macbeth
ColorChecker and fitting the in-scene high-pressure-sodium streetlamps (whose
halos already sit in the baseline frame at $R/G=1.84$, $B/G=0.08$) would
directly measure this specific unit's CCM and exact 656~nm red transmission.
\emph{(iii)~A fourth band.} Narrowband filters centered at 427.8~nm (\Ntp{})
and 656.3~nm (H$\alpha$), or a second camera with a steeper 650~nm IR-cut,
turn $w_m$ and H$\alpha$ into direct photometry.
\emph{(iv)~Time resolution.} Continuum and N\,\textsc{ii} decay in tens of
microseconds, H$\alpha$ persists longer, and molecular bands ride leaders and
continuing currents; even 1000~fps video separates hot strokes from continuing
afterglows.
\emph{(v)~Ensembles.} Across many flashes at varying distances (ideally paired
with lightning-location network ranges), regressing chromaticity against range
separates intrinsic source color from Rayleigh and aerosol path reddening.
\emph{(vi)~A transmission grating.} Placing a blazed grating over a spare
camera converts the monitor into a slitless spectrograph, replacing template
inversion with a direct spectrum.

\section{Engineering: cloud, air, and silicon}
\label{sec:engineering}
\textbf{Water and air.} The flash reached the camera by diffusion through the
overcast deck. Cloud droplets ($\sim$10~$\mu$m) scatter visible light in the
geometric-optics regime with single-scatter albedo $\omega_0\approx 1$ and
negligible wavelength dependence; multiple scattering randomizes direction
and smears arrival time (by tens of microseconds per kilometer of diffusion
path) while remaining nearly chromatically neutral
\citep{thomasonkrider1982,koshak1994,bohren1987}. Liquid- and vapor-phase
water absorption across 400--700~nm is negligible over these path lengths.
Because lossless elastic scattering by a neutral cloud deck at midnight cannot
create white light (ambient skyglow is dim sodium-amber, $40\times$ fainter
than the flash), the cloud preserves the source chromaticity; the lavender is
pale (CIE excitation purity $\approx 23\%$) purely because the lightning
spectrum itself is broadband. Between the cloud deck and the camera we include
clear-air Rayleigh extinction over an assumed 3~km effective path,
$\tau(\lambda)=0.036\,(550/\lambda)^4$, noting that a more distant cell
($\sim$10~km path, or humid summer boundary-layer aerosols) adds stronger blue
attenuation that shifts the inferred continuum toward the bluer Planck side
(\S\ref{sec:fit}).

\textbf{Silicon and ISP.} The camera (Elgato Facecam~4K, Sony STARVIS~2
IMX678-class BSI CMOS sensor) is operated in manual exposure and gain with
white balance fixed at 5600~K. We approximate the raw Bayer passbands as
Gaussians (B:~455/38~nm, G:~535/45~nm, R:~612/48~nm) within the
\citet{jiang2013} camera family, multiplied by a 670~nm IR-cut and 398~nm UV
edge. At H$\alpha$ (656.3~nm), the Gaussian red curve ($0.653$) times the
IR-cut transmission ($0.907$) retains $0.59$ of peak sensitivity---high enough
for H$\alpha$ to lift $R$ to parity with $G$, whereas a sharper 650~nm IR-cut
would render the flash noticeably bluer. As shown in \S\ref{sec:obs} and
\S\ref{sec:fit}, two ISP stages beyond raw Bayer integration govern the
radiometry: (1)~the $3\times 3$ CCM that decorrelates the overlapping Bayer
filters into linear sRGB, and (2)~the nonlinear per-channel tone curve, whose
empirical calibration eliminates brightness-dependent $R/G$ drift and exposes
the highlight shoulder above DN~220.

\section{Meteorology}
The synoptic context was a warm, humid, overcast boundary layer. In the
monitor's local weather log (\code{science.csv}), Pittsburgh sat at
73--74$^\circ$F, 94--100\% overcast, and 81--85\% surface relative humidity
across 00:00--01:00~EDT (rising to 94\% by 01:44~EDT), with light drizzle
noted at 23:29~EDT; ERA5 reanalysis similarly places a saturated low overcast
deck (86--90\% surface humidity) and regional drizzle over the area without
flagging a grid-point thunderstorm \citep{hersbach2020}. The flash almost
certainly came from an isolated convective cell embedded within or beyond the
stratiform deck, of the sub-grid scale reanalysis routinely smooths away. Two
weaker blue sky excursions at 00:33 and 00:39~EDT (22 and 28~minutes after the
main frame) are consistent with the same small cell passing at greater
distance. The humid overcast mattered twice: abundant water vapor fed the
mandatory H$\alpha$ line (\S\ref{sec:fit}), and the low cloud deck provided
the hemispheric projection screen. The capture is also a one-frame
demonstration of fixed-exposure urban sky monitoring: a 100~ms exposure every
$\sim$326~s is a $\sim$1-in-3260 duty cycle, yet over a season of nights even
brief transients land in frames whose radiometry remains quantitatively
interpretable after the fact.

\section{\texorpdfstring{N\,\textsc{ii}, \Ntp{}, and the ecosystem: the fertilizer in the spectrum}{N II, N2+, and the ecosystem: the fertilizer in the spectrum}}
\label{sec:ecosystem}
The nitrogen emission of the flash points at the atmosphere's strongest
natural machine for breaking the N$\equiv$N triple bond. At 9.8~eV, that bond
is why nitrogen---the most abundant element in the atmosphere---remains a
limiting nutrient across ecosystems until something tears it open. Lightning
operates two distinct plasma regimes that break N$_2$, and our spectrum speaks
to both.

The dominant factory is thermal, and its optical certificate is the
\emph{atomic} spectrum. Every N\,\textsc{ii} photon attests that an
N$\equiv$N bond was broken and the fragment ionized besides. The actual
fixing, however, happens in the dark. In the $\sim$30\,000~K core, nitric oxide
cannot survive: it rapidly re-equilibrates back to N$_2$ and O$_2$ as the gas
expands. It is the 2000--4000~K cooling shock shell and turbulent mixing zone
that runs the thermal Zel'dovich chain
($\mathrm{O + N_2 \rightleftharpoons NO + N}$ and
$\mathrm{N + O_2 \rightleftharpoons NO + O}$, operating on ground-state
$\mathrm{N(^4S)}$), freezing NO out of equilibrium as the gas cools below
$\sim$2500~K \citep{zeldovich1966,borucki1984}. That shell is
spectroscopically silent in the visible: far too cool to excite N\,\textsc{ii}
or near-infrared N\,\textsc{i} lines, while low-lying forbidden atomic
transitions (such as the [N\,\textsc{i}] 520~nm doublet of metastable
$\mathrm{N(^2D)}$) are both Boltzmann-suppressed in thermal gas and
collisionally quenched at tropospheric pressure. The core that shines does not
fix; the shell that fixes does not shine.

The second regime is cold, non-equilibrium plasma---the corona sheath,
leaders, and streamers where any \Ntp{} first-negative emission originates
(\S\ref{sec:molecular}). Ionizing N$_2$ to \Ntp{} leaves the molecule intact
(bond order 2.5, dissociation energy 8.7~eV). In the tenuous thermosphere,
dissociative recombination ($\mathrm{N_2^+ + e^- \to N + N}$, branching
heavily to metastable $\mathrm{N(^2D)}$, which reacts rapidly with O$_2$ to
form NO) turns aurorae into a major upper-atmospheric NO source
\citep{rees1989,barth1992}. In dense 1-atm tropospheric air, however, ion
kinetics shift sharply: three-body clustering
($\mathrm{N_2^+ + N_2 + M \to N_4^+ + M}$) and rapid charge transfer to
lower-ionization-potential oxygen
($\mathrm{N_2^+/N_4^+ + O_2 \to O_2^+ + N_2}$) occur within nanoseconds,
outcompeting direct \Ntp{} dissociative recombination by orders of magnitude.
Cold-plasma nitrogen fixation in tropospheric streamers and corona therefore
proceeds mainly via direct electron-impact dissociation of neutral N$_2$
($\mathrm{e^- + N_2 \to e^- + N + N}$), with any \Ntp{} violet emission
serving as an energetic-electron tracer of that cold-plasma envelope rather
than the primary chemical intermediate itself. Our upper bound
($w_m \le 0.14$, compatible with zero) aligns with the global NO$_x$ budget,
which is overwhelmingly dominated by the hot-channel thermal factory.

That budget is ecologically substantial. A single flash fixes of order a few
kilograms of nitrogen; globally, lightning produces roughly
2--8~Tg~N~yr$^{-1}$, several percent of natural fixation and the dominant
source of NO$_x$ in the free troposphere, where it regulates ozone production
\citep{borucki1984,schumann2007}. Before biological nitrogenase evolved, such
abiotic channels supplied early life; models of the Archaean atmosphere
suggest declining CO$_2$ could have throttled lightning fixation severely
enough to create a nitrogen crisis \citep{navarrogonzalez2001}. In the
modern atmosphere, lightning NO oxidizes within minutes via ozone to
NO$_2$---observed as column enhancements under storms since
\citet{noxon1976}---and then, over timescales of hours at night (via
$\mathrm{NO_2 + O_3 \to NO_3}$, $\mathrm{N_2O_5}$ formation, and heterogeneous
hydrolysis on cloud droplets and aerosols \cite{seinfeld2016}), converts to
nitric acid and scavenges into precipitation as nitrate. Over the course of
the wet overnight system, storm-fixed nitrate washed down onto the regional
watershed and the campus lawns in the image foreground---the same lawns whose
green chromatic coordinate this instrument logs daily as a phenology metric.
In a single 100~ms frame, the monitor photographed the atmospheric machinery
that helps fertilize its own field of view.

\section{Aesthetics and color theory}
In CIE~1931 chromaticity coordinates, the baseline-subtracted flash sits at
$(x,y)=(0.273,0.261)$: an excitation purity of just $23\%$ and a dominant
wavelength of $\lambda_d\approx 462$~nm (indigo--blue-violet, right at the
short-wavelength shoulder of the spectrum locus bordering the non-spectral
line of purples \cite{wyszecki1982}). Yet physically the flash is not a
462~nm narrowband source mixed with white light: it is a \emph{metamer},
built from a broadband continuum plus nitrogen's blue--violet emission at one
end and hydrogen's red H$\alpha$ at the other, bracketing a green minimum.
When H$\alpha$ is even stronger (or the continuum redder), lightning pushes
fully past 380~nm onto the non-spectral line of purples---which is why
lightning has read as violet or lavender to observers since long before CCDs,
and why daylight-balanced film renders it the same way
\citep{minnaert1954,salanave1961}. Goethe, wrong about mechanism and acute
about phenomenology, placed \emph{Purpur} at the summit of his color system:
the union of the two intensified poles of his polarity, a color nature
produces only under extremity \citep{goethe1810}. The lightning spectrum is
about as literal an enactment of that figure as physics offers---the two ends
of the visible conspiring against the middle.

The frame's beauty is also relational, and here the French tradition earns its
keep. \citet{chevreul1839}, codifying simultaneous contrast for the Gobelins
dyers, showed that complementary colors mutually exalt one another; our
archive applies his law diachronically. The flash frame sits in a season of
sodium-amber nights (\code{\#1c1808}); amber and indigo--violet are near
complements, so the anomalous frame does not merely differ from its
neighbors---it negates them, and each makes the other more vivid, the eye
carrying the remembered amber into the lavender. \citet{albers1963} made the
same point operational: color is never absolute, and the identical pixel
triplet pasted into a daylight scene would read as pale cool grey.
\citet{wittgenstein1977} circles a related puzzle---whatever looks luminous
cannot look grey---and our sky obliges: values that would be a dull mauve on
paper look radiant solely because the surround is midnight. As for what the
photograph \emph{is}: in Barthes's terms the thousands of rows of the season's
log are \emph{studium}, and the one lavender row is \emph{punctum}, the detail
that pierces; his \emph{\c{c}a-a-\'et\'e}---the photograph's stubborn
``that-has-been''---acquires an edge here, because this particular
that-has-been lasted 100~ms and was witnessed by no one \citep{barthes1980}.
\citet{merleauponty1945} held that color is not a property but an encounter
between a lived body and the world; this color was encountered by an apparatus
alone, and became a percept only in retrospect, a memory without a rememberer.
\citet{kandinsky1911} heard violet as a cooled red, ``morbid,
extinguished''---the thermodynamic pun is free of charge---and
\citet{pastoureau2000,pastoureau2008} reminds us violet (long read as
\emph{subniger}, half-black) served centuries as \emph{demi-deuil},
half-mourning: for a tenth of a second, the sky over Pittsburgh wore it.

\section{Holes in the theory}
\label{sec:holes}
In decreasing order of concern:
(1)~\emph{Exact unit CCM, white point, and IR-cut.} Daytime archive skies
validate a colorimetrically accurate sRGB pipeline, but the overcast median
($B/G=1.32$ vs.\ $1.16$ for 6500~K at nominal 5600~K WB) hints at either a
slightly cooler effective white balance ($\sim$5200~K), bluer overcast clouds
($\sim$7000~K), or mild ISP chroma boost; likewise, the exact red response at
656.3~nm controls how much H$\alpha$ is needed. A reference ColorChecker shot
would pin down both directly.
(2)~\emph{Continuum opacity/temperature versus path length.} With two color
ratios, trading an optically thin Kramers continuum (3~km path) against a
bluer continuum reddened by a longer clear-air/aerosol path ($\sim$10~km) is
unresolved without an independent range estimate from a lightning-location
network.
(3)~\emph{Stroke-phase and flash-type variation.} Our 100~ms exposure
integrates hot return-stroke cores and cool afterglows in unknown proportion,
and flash type (intracloud versus cloud-to-ground) carries systematically
different spectral energetics \citep{rakovuman2003,walker2019}.
(4)~\emph{Thin propagation model.} Cloud diffusion is treated as strictly
achromatic, neglecting weak droplet/aerosol absorption over multi-kilometer
multiple-scattering paths \citep{thomasonkrider1982}.
(5)~\emph{Single event and highlight compression.} With $n=1$, 8-bit JPEG
quantization, and blue-channel highlight roll-off above DN~220
(Fig.~\ref{fig:radiometry}b), the unclipped sky band carries a $\sim$5\%
systematic bracket on $B/G$. Item~(1) can be retired cheaply with an in-scene
color target; items~(2) and (3) invite range-matched flash ensembles or a
transmission grating.
